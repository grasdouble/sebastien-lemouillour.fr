A chatbot helped you draft an email, your phone unlocked by recognizing your face, and a music app queued up the next perfect track without you touching a single setting. If you've found yourself wondering what is actually happening under the hood, you're asking exactly the right question.

### "AI" Is Not One Thing

Artificial Intelligence, or AI, is an umbrella term for computer systems designed to perform tasks that would otherwise require human-like reasoning: understanding language, recognizing images, or making decisions from incomplete data. The [OECD defines AI](https://oecd.ai/en/wonk/ai-definitions) as "a machine-based system that can, for a given set of objectives, make predictions, recommendations, or decisions influencing real and virtual environments." That definition is deliberately broad, because AI covers a huge family of approaches.

It helps to think in two levels. **Narrow AI** (also called weak AI) does one well-defined thing well: spam detection, voice transcription, recommendation engines. Every AI product you interact with today falls here. **Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)**, the kind that reasons flexibly across any domain the way a human does, does not exist yet; it remains an active research goal and a heavily debated concept.

### How AI Differs from Regular Software

Traditional software follows explicit rules: if the temperature sensor reads over 80°C, send an alert. The programmer specifies every decision. AI inverts this: instead of writing the rules, you feed the system examples and let it discover patterns. Show it thousands of spam emails alongside thousands of legitimate ones, and it builds its own internal logic for telling them apart.

This shift from rule-based to pattern-based thinking is what makes AI feel almost uncanny. It's also why AI can surprise you, for better (catching fraud patterns a human analyst missed) and for worse (confidently stating something incorrect).

### Where the Term Came From

The word was coined at the [1956 Dartmouth Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_workshop), when researchers proposed that "every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it." Decades of booms and AI winters followed. The current wave is driven by three forces arriving at once: massive datasets, cheap computing power, and algorithmic breakthroughs, particularly [neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_neural_network), loosely modeled on the brain.

### What This Means for You

AI is not magic, and it will not replace human judgment wholesale. It is a powerful pattern-matching tool that works best when paired with human oversight. The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence) specifically calls for human accountability in AI systems, because patterns learned from data can reflect the biases and gaps in that data.

If you take one thing from this guide: AI systems learn from examples, not instructions. That single idea explains most of what makes them impressive and most of what makes them fail.
